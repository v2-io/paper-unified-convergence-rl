---
title: A Unified Convergence Theory for Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning
authors:
  - name: Joseph A. Wecker
    affiliation: Independent

---

Non-stationary reinforcement learning lacks a unified convergence theory: variation-budget regret bounds, sliding-window methods, tempo-and-forgetting analyses, two-term decompositions, and causal-RL frameworks each address fragments without composing. We present, to our knowledge, the first *compatible bundle of guarantees* that delivers all three properties — non-stationarity persistence, explicit metric structure on policy space, and on-policy learnability — *jointly*. Each of four components supplies a distinct epistemic property: (i) a two-gap diagnostic separating goal-feasibility from policy-quality routes corrective action; (ii) a point-mass reverse-KL/TV identity under deterministic optimum, $\mathrm{TV}(\delta_{a^*}, Q) = 1 - e^{-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\delta_{a^*} \,\|\, Q)}$ — an *exact* algebraic identity that lies strictly below the Pinsker and Bretagnolle--Huber envelopes at this corner — supplies a coordinate-optimal regret coordinate; (iii) a multi-factor strategic tempo $\mathcal{T}_\Sigma = \min_{(i,j)} \nu_{ij} \cdot \iota_{ij} \cdot (1-\lambda_{ij})$ with structural survival inequality $\mathcal{T}_\Sigma > \rho_\Sigma/R_\Sigma$ supplies persistence; (iv) closed-loop interventional access under sequential ignorability supplies on-policy learnability via the Pearl causal hierarchy. Composition yields a Best-of-Both-Worlds dynamic regret theorem $\tilde O(\min\{V_{\max} N_h \sqrt{(B_T+1)T},\ V_{\max} N_h (V_T+1)^{1/3} T^{2/3}\})$ — automatically adapting between piecewise-stationary segment count $B_T$ and continuous variation budget $V_T$ without prior knowledge of either; the $V_T$ exponent is information-theoretically near-optimal at the deterministic-$\pi^*$ corner. A structural failure-class theorem identifies the *gain-decay* updates (count-accumulating Bayesian schemes, Robbins--Monro gradient methods) as universally violating the forgetting prerequisite for any positive disturbance rate; finite-gain mechanisms (constant-step stochastic approximation, sliding windows, bounded memory, block restart) face *bidirectional* thresholds instead. The deterministic-optimum scope extends perturbatively to $\epsilon$-stochastic and softmax-regularized policies with $O(\epsilon\log(1/\epsilon))$ and $O(\tau^{-1}\exp(-\Delta_{\min}/\tau))$ corrections (exponentially small with polynomial prefactor). Theory only; we make the ProST reduction rigorous at the steady-state sector-parameter level, lifting it from a single-factor hyperparameter claim to a multi-factor structural-threshold claim.
