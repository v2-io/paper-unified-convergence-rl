## Prior-Art Retrieval Summary ^sec-prior-art

We conducted a structured prior-art retrieval (via Undermind) with the goal of identifying any framework composing all four target elements: (1) goal-feasibility-vs-policy-quality two-gap diagnostic; (2) an exact reverse-KL/TV regret identity (point-mass) under deterministic-π*, or — more loosely — Bretagnolle–Huber-style use in regret analysis; (3) strategic-tempo / forgetting prerequisite tying convergence to rate of useful policy revision; (4) closed-loop interventional access making regret bounds learnable from on-policy data.

**Result: 63 papers retrieved, abstract-level coverage estimated 75%, no direct anticipation in the retrieved set.** The landscape splits into four largely separate strands corresponding to our four components; we did not find a published framework composing all four in this retrieval. Retrieval queries used the structured prior-art tool Undermind; the four-strand cite-and-distinguish in [[#^sec-related-work]] surfaces the closest neighbor in each strand.

**Strongest negative signal (in the retrieved corpus): the Bretagnolle–Huber inequality is absent as an upper-bound coordinate for regret analysis in the retrieved RL / non-stationary literature.** The information-theoretic regret line — \cite{russo-2014-informationtheoretic, russo-vanroy-2014-ids, lu-2019-informationtheoretic, min-russo-2023-nonstat-bandit, lattimore-2021-mirror, kakade-2020-information-online} — uses entropy / mutual information / information ratio / Pinsker / Hellinger; we did not find the BH inequality deployed *as an upper-bound regret coordinate*. (BH is well-known in bandit *lower-bound* proofs via Le Cam's method and Tsybakov's framework \cite{tsybakov-2009-nonparametric}, including in standard RL textbook treatments \cite{lattimore-2020-bandit}; that use is not contested here.) The *exact point-mass identity at the deterministic-π* corner* ([[#^lem-pointmass-identity]]) — which strictly improves both Pinsker and the BH envelope at this corner — was likewise not found in the retrieved corpus, in either upper-bound or lower-bound use. We frame this as a literature-search observation against an explicitly bounded retrieval (63 papers, ~75% abstract-level coverage), not a formal absence claim against all published RL work; the claim is best read as "to our knowledge / in the retrieved set" rather than "absolutely novel."

**Closest neighbors per strand:** \cite{cheung-simchi-levi-zhu-2022-blessing} (Strand 1 — variation-budget dynamic regret); \cite{li-zhao-zhou-2024-dynamic-regret} (Strand 2 — closest two-term decomposition, different axis); \cite{lee-2023-prost-tempo} (Strand 3 — closest tempo result, different form); \cite{zhang-2022-online-rl} (Strand 4 — closest causal-RL, stationary only).
